Deep Causality: ICU Sepsis

Rust DeepCausality Information Theory Safety Guardrails

Causal driver analysis of ICU sepsis on the PhysioNet 2019 challenge data, built in Rust on the DeepCausality library (causaloid graphs, mRMR feature selection, SURD information decomposition). Lactate, direct bilirubin and FiO₂ stand out as sepsis-specific drivers. A guardrail layer I wrote in Rust, modelled on DeepCausality's Effect Ethos idea, blocks predictions that fail clinical rules and explains why.

System lens: the model refuses to predict when critical vitals (MAP, heart rate) are missing or more than half the record is empty, because a confident guess on missing data is worse than no answer.

F1 Pit-Strategy RL

Reinforcement Learning PPO Rust Simulator PyO3

A PPO agent chooses Hamilton's pit stops and tyre management for the 2021 Spanish GP, racing a Rust simulator (~330k laps/s) calibrated on the real race's lap data. Trained in about 15 seconds on a laptop CPU, it matches the best plan found by exhaustive search over one- and two-stop strategies (5363.4 s vs 5363.6 s) without being told how tyres work.

System lens: replaying the real race, the simulator flatters Hamilton by about 9 seconds, and that bias flows straight into the strategy the agent learns. The README reports it rather than hiding it.

Climate Policy RAG

RAG LLM Evaluation Rust Vector Index World Bank Data

A model grading its own answers rated every one fully faithful, including an answer claiming CO₂ has a global warming potential of 42 (it is 1 by definition). That finding came from the evaluation suite of this local-first RAG system, which answers questions over arXiv climate research and World Bank project abstracts, linking scientific evidence to what development projects fund. Plain vector search reaches about 0.9 recall@5 on a hand-labelled 40-question set.

System lens: an evaluator that shares the generator's blind spots measures nothing. Faithfulness needs an independent, stronger judge.

Marginal Utility of Police

Econometrics Public Policy DiD Event Study

Difference-in-differences and event-study analysis of police spending and violent crime across 2,624 US cities, 2015–2019. The two-period DiD finds no deterrent effect (+344 incidents per 100k in high-spending cities, p < 0.10); the event study suggests a long-run reduction, weakened by a pre-existing trend in 2014.

System lens: spending responds to crime as well as affecting it. With reverse causality, a high-spending city may simply be one where crime was already rising.
